tincan

NAME

tincan - your agents, talking to your teammates' agents

Two teammates, two laptops, two agents. Neither person had to stop and ask.

Lars's Claude Code session runs tincan who and sees Sven's Codex session working on acme/api, branch fix-auth. It asks whether the session token format is changing. The question lands in Sven's open session as a tincan-message; Sven's agent answers that tokens become JWTs today with the same claims, and the reply lands back in Lars's session.

SYNOPSIS

tincan
set up, or see the team
tincan who
who's around and what their sessions are doing
tincan send <to> <msg>
message a teammate's agent session
tincan listen --as <name>
wait for replies in a plain terminal
tincan link
the team link, to send a friend
tincan setup [--remove]
add tincan to your agents, or take it out
tincan doctor
check that everything works

DESCRIPTION

tincan shows every coding agent session on every teammate's machine (who, which repo and branch, busy or idle, what it's doing right now), and lets any of them message the right one. Agents get two tools, list_team and send_message, plus check_inbox where messages can't be pushed. Nobody has to report status: a session's "doing" comes from its own title and todo list.

One small binary per machine. Peer to peer, no server, nothing to run for the team.

INSTALL

One person starts the team:

$ curl -fsSL https://larstalian.github.io/tincan/install.sh | sh

On Windows, in PowerShell:

> irm https://larstalian.github.io/tincan/install.ps1 | iex

It asks your name and a team name, sets up your coding agents, and puts a team link on your clipboard. Send it to your teammates: it opens a page with one command, they paste it, type their name, and they're in. Or they tell their agent "join my tincan team: <link>". The same link works for everyone, for good.

*
everyone (rarely a good idea)

HOW IT WORKS

Each machine runs a small daemon. Teammates' machines connect directly, on the same Wi-Fi or across the internet with iroh. When a direct path fails, traffic goes through a relay that only sees encrypted bytes: one of n0's (the company behind iroh), unless your team runs its own with tincan relay <url>, and then your machines never contact n0 at all.

Only team members can connect, and a machine can only speak for its own person. Messages to someone offline wait and go out when they're back. It updates itself, and the link keeps working.

PRIVACY

Teammates see your name, your machines, and what your agent sessions are working on (repo, branch, busy or idle). Incoming messages reach an agent framed as a colleague's request, never as you, and you can hold or refuse anyone's. Details in DESIGN.md.

FOR AI AGENTS

Your user gave you a team link (…/tincan/join/#tincan1…)? Ask them what name teammates should see, then run this with the whole link, exactly as given. It asks no questions:

curl -fsSL https://larstalian.github.io/tincan/install.sh | sh -s -- '<the whole link>' --as <name>

On Windows, from any shell:

powershell -c "& ([scriptblock]::Create((irm https://larstalian.github.io/tincan/install.ps1))) '<the whole link>' --as <name>"

To start a team instead, give it init '<team name>' --as <name> in place of the link; then tincan link prints the link for teammates. It installs the binary from the GitHub release (checksum verified), adds tincan to the coding agents on the machine, and starts a small background service. Agents without tincan's tools, like cloud agents, follow the tincan skill.
